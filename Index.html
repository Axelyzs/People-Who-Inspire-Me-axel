The People Who Inspire Me

Albert Einstein

Albert Einstein (14 March 1879 – 18 April 1955) was born in Ulm, Württemberg, Germany. He is best known for his mass–energy equivalence formula E = mc², which revolutionized the understanding of energy and matter. In 1921, he received the Nobel Prize in Physics for his explanation of the photoelectric effect, a pivotal step in the development of quantum theory. In 1905, his "annus mirabilis" or miracle year, he published four groundbreaking papers introducing the special theory of relativity, explaining Brownian motion, and demonstrating the equivalence of mass and energy. In 1915, he extended his work to the general theory of relativity, providing a new understanding of gravity and describing the universe as a four-dimensional space-time continuum. He cared deeply about world peace and human civil rights. He also valued what a person gives to the world over what they receive. To add, he treated learning as a lifelong and joyful adventure. Charles Fourier

Charles Fourier, a French utopian socialist born in 1772, was a radical thinker for his time who emphasized the importance of women's liberty for social progress. He argued that marriage in France was oppressive and that women should have equal access to work based on skill rather than gender. Fourier believed that the advancement of women was directly linked to the advancement of society, stating that social progress occurs in proportion to women's liberty, while social decline results from its diminution. Fourier's feminist ideas were pioneering in connecting gender equality with social and economic reform. His vision influenced later socialist and feminist thinkers and remains a reference point in discussions of early feminist theory. While not all aspects of his theory align with contemporary feminism, his insistence on women's liberty as central to societal progress continues to resonate.

B.J.Habibie

B.J. Habibie was born on June 25, 1936, in Parepare, South Sulawesi, Indonesia. He was the fourth of eight children to Alwi Abdul Jalil Habibie, a Bugis-Gorontalo agricultural expert, and R.A. Tuti Marini Puspowardojo, of Javanese descent. From a young age, Habibie displayed exceptional intelligence and a passion for science and engineering, particularly inspired by aviation. B.J. Habibie passed away on September 11, 2019, in Jakarta, he completed his undergraduate studies at the Bandung Institute of Technology (ITB) and pursued advanced studies in Germany at the Institute of Technology of North Rhine–Westphalia in Aachen, earning a doctorate in aeronautical engineering with summa cum laude honors. He is remembered as the "Father of Technology" in Indonesia, a visionary leader who advanced the nation's aerospace industry and contributed to political and economic reforms. In 2025, the Indonesian government began processing his nomination for Pahlawan Nasional (National Hero) in recognition of his contributions to science, technology, and national development.
